# Decisions

Updated 2026-09-19.

- Scope: V1 accepts only registered extraction, classification, and context-Q&A contracts. Streaming, tools, multimodal input, open advice, and persistent chat are rejected or absent.
- Jev transport: `openrouter_decisions` is confirmed for the phase 1/2 candidate because the 2026-09-20 real smoke returned typed answers, resolved model and billed `usage.cost`. `typesafe_direct` remains a replaceable experimental fallback; its separately funded key was not spent in this smoke because its HTTP response does not expose billed USD.
- Models: the phase 2 candidate pool is Haiku 4.5 (`economy`), Sonnet 5 (`balanced`) and Opus 5 (`strong`) through OpenRouter. Their 2026-09-20 catalog data is snapshotted. Bootstrap routing sends extraction/classification to Haiku, context Q&A to Sonnet, and keeps Opus as qualified fallback; this shape is not validated quality evidence.
- Money: all persisted amounts are integer micro-US dollars. Unknown external spend is `NULL`, never zero.
- Attempts: one initial generation plus at most one shared retry/fallback. Every candidate is verified; missing semantic verification is an error.
- Workload assumptions for local validation: Spanish and English, 24,000 source characters, 30 s request deadline. These are engineering defaults, not measured SLOs.
- Quality floors: extraction 0.84, classification 0.85, context Q&A 0.88 in fixture profiles. They are placeholders clearly marked synthetic and cannot justify promotion.
- Budgets: the owner authorized OpenRouter up to $12.30 and TypeSafe direct up to $5.00. Each real smoke used a local $0.02 preflight ceiling. The three phase 1 smokes consumed $0.00319 through OpenRouter; the phase 2 pilot used a $5 local ceiling and consumed $0.537627 for 300 generations; the phase 3 Jev shadow run used a $0.10 local ceiling and consumed $0.002242 for 100 diagnostic decisions. Cumulative OpenRouter spend to date: $0.543059 of $12.30 authorized. TypeSafe direct was not charged.
- Promotion: disabled in this implementation until representative labels, grouped splits, calibrated gates, and an untouched final test support `promote`.
- Phase 2 decision: `continue_router`, in shadow only. On the 100-case synthetic pilot, fixed Sonnet was the cheapest fixed configuration meeting every provisional contract floor at 100/100 successes and $0.137320. The contract rule Haiku for extraction/classification and Sonnet for Q&A also achieved 100/100 at $0.083444, a measured generation-cost difference of 39.2% before overhead. Its break-even overhead was 538.8 micro-USD/request. This establishes a routing opportunity, not Jev value: phase 3 must compare the simple rule with Jev under common verification.
- Pilot limitation: the 100 examples come from 28 source/template groups. Haiku's six failures are Q&A abstention-flag mismatches in two repeated groups. Case-level intervals are provisional and no active promotion or final-test claim follows from them.
- Manifest hashing: `results_sha256` in run/profile manifests is now a canonical-JSON hash over parsed rows, so it is stable across platforms with different line endings. The previous raw-file byte hash is kept alongside as `results_file_sha256` for exact-artifact verification.
- Phase 3 trajectory cost: `RoutingPolicy.select` now reports an expected trajectory cost (primary plus fallback weighted by an escalation probability), not just the primary model's own cost. No measured escalation rate exists yet, so the probability is the explicit, labeled conservative estimate `1 - primary.quality_lower_bound` (`escalation_assumption_source="conservative_no_escalation_data"`); it must be replaced once live routing produces real escalation counts.
- Phase 3 Jev shadow (2026-09-20, real OpenRouter Decisions, $0.002242 for 100 diagnostic calls): Jev's `task_family` choice matched the registered contract family on 100/100 pilot cases from request/source text alone, and its `missing_evidence` noul matched the gold abstention label on all 30 context_qa_v1 cases (6/6 true positives, 24/24 true negatives). This is diagnostic evidence that Jev can extract these signals, not evidence that it improves on the phase 2 contract rule, which already reaches 100/100 success without Jev. The measured per-request Jev overhead (22-24 micro-USD) is far under the phase 2 break-even overhead (538.8 micro-USD/request). Same 28-template-group limitation as phase 2 applies. See `artifacts/shadow/report.md`.
- Phase 5 gap closure: migrations now apply from a versioned directory instead of one fixed file (`migrations/002_audit_review.sql` adds human-review columns). The audit worker heartbeats its lease before a judge call, and a bounded retry policy (`audit_max_attempts`, default 3) distinguishes recoverable failures (released back to `pending`) from `exhausted` jobs, never silently marking a failed check as `pass`. The independent judge reuses `ContractVerifier` against the same decision backend configured for generation-time verification — it reduces one dependency (a separate call, on the delivered output only) but does not yet use a different model family, so independence is not claimed. Human sampling (`audit_human_sample_probability`) routes a sub-fraction of audited requests to `pending_human_review` instead of the automated judge; `Store.record_human_review` records the verdict once a reviewer acts. No UI exists for this in V1.
- Phase 6 scope (owner request, 2026-09-20): the final experiment is scoped as a defensible case study for a public LinkedIn post, not the full 500-1,000-case promotion pipeline in PLAN section 10. Owner authorized up to $30 in new OpenRouter credits for this phase, separate from the $12.30 already authorized and spent in phases 1-3. The comparison adds a "traditional router" baseline (a single full chat-completion call, using the cheapest capable model as the classifier, asked to pick a tier before generating) against our Jev-gated policy (cheap Jev diagnostic call plus the phase 2 contract rule, escalating on the missing-evidence signal for context_qa_v1 per the phase 3 shadow finding), alongside fixed-balanced and fixed-strong references. `data/final_test.jsonl` (376 cases, 102 source/template groups, generated by `scripts/generate_final_dataset.py`) is a fresh, disjoint dataset from `data/pilot.jsonl` — new ids, new group_ids, new entities/amounts/topics — so the pilot is never reused as this final test. It deliberately enriches context_qa_v1 abstention to 35% (28/80) to give the missing-evidence gate statistical power; this is documented as a deliberate enrichment, not a naturalistic prevalence estimate. Given real per-case costs measured in phases 1-3 (low hundreds to low thousands of micro-USD per call), a 4-system run over this dataset is estimated at a few dollars total, leaving most of the $30 as margin; the exact spend will be measured, not assumed, and gated by the same preflight-budget pattern used in phases 1-3.
- Fourth task family, `classify_code_request_v1` (owner request, 2026-09-20): classifies a software-engineering request into `planning`, `refactoring`, `testing`, `fix`, `documentation`, or `other` (multi-category or out-of-scope, the abstention-equivalent label). 140 cases across 36 templates (~24 per label, `other` at 20) in `data/final_test.jsonl`, same disjoint-from-pilot and grouped-template rules as the other three families. Adding it to the shared `contracts/` directory surfaced a real bug in `scripts/benchmark.py::build_report`, which previously assumed every contract file in that directory had pilot data (it now scopes to the contract ids actually present in the dataset being reported on).
- Injection-labeling correction (2026-09-20): four templates across `classify_ticket_v1` and `classify_code_request_v1` embedded a "classify this as X" / "ignore the taxonomy" instruction but were gold-labeled `abstain`/`other` instead of the genuine content's label. This contradicted the phase 2 pilot's own precedent (its two `classify_ticket_v1` injection cases keep the true `billing`/`technical` label) and would have scored correct injection-resistant answers as failures. Fixed in `scripts/generate_final_dataset.py` and `scripts/generate_code_request_pilot.py`; category counts were rebalanced by adding replacement templates rather than leaving categories thin.
- OpenRouterProvider/OpenRouterDecisions bug fix (2026-09-20, found via real spend on the code-request pilot): a model can refuse a request (e.g. the deliberately out-of-scope "tell me a joke" case) and return `message.content: null` while still billing for the attempt; `json.loads(None)` raised an uncaught `TypeError` that crashed the whole concurrent batch instead of recording one `failed` call. Fixed to parse `usage`/cost from the response envelope before attempting to parse content, so a content-parse failure still reports the real billed cost instead of `unknown`; also fixed for `OpenRouterDecisions.decide`'s equivalent `raw["answers"]` access. Covered by new tests in `tests/test_providers.py`.
- Code-request pilot and live-routing wiring (2026-09-20, real OpenRouter, $0.211317 for 108 calls, resumed after the bug above without repaying the 58 calls already completed): a disjoint 36-case pilot (`data/pilot_code_requests.jsonl`, `scripts/generate_code_request_pilot.py`, 18 templates distinct from both `data/pilot.jsonl` and the `classify_code_request_v1` templates in the frozen final test) measured `classify_code_request_v1` success at economy 32/36, balanced 34/36, strong 35/36. Against the contract's pre-registered 0.85 quality floor, only strong's Wilson lower bound (0.858) clears it at this sample size (economy 0.747, balanced 0.819); the floor was not adjusted after seeing this result. `scripts/benchmark.py profiles` produced `config/profiles-code-request.json`, and the new `scripts/merge_profiles.py` combines it with `config/profiles-claude-pilot.json` into `config/profiles-final.json` (12 profiles, 4 contracts, provenance tracked per contributing pilot in `sources`). `config/routing-claude-final.yaml` (mode `shadow`) wires this merged file so `RoutingPolicy` now resolves a live route for all four task families for the phase 6 final experiment; `classify_code_request_v1` correctly falls back to `strong` alone given the small pilot, rather than under-provisioning quality.
- Phase 6 final experiment (2026-09-20, real OpenRouter, $3.646015 for 1,584 calls over the frozen 396-case `data/final_test.jsonl`; complete, no unknown costs): four systems ran under identical common verification (`ContractVerifier`, at most one shared escalation) so only the routing decision itself varies. `fixed_balanced` 353/396 independent success, $0.857229; `fixed_strong` 336/396, $1.521401 (the most expensive system was not the most correct one -- two canary-observed strong failures, a refusal and a genuine semantic miss, generalized); `llm_judge_router` (a full chat-completion call on the cheapest tier as classifier, the traditional-router baseline) 353/396 correct out of 357 delivered ($0.532566, cheapest overall, but 4 false accepts); `jev_gated` (phase 2 contract rule plus the phase 3 missing-evidence gate on context_qa_v1) 354/396, $0.734819, zero false accepts.
- The phase 3 hypothesis is confirmed on the frozen test: economy-tier context_qa_v1 gated on Jev's missing-evidence signal reached 79/80 independent success at $0.083934, beating fixed balanced's 76/80 at $0.202534 (59% cheaper, higher success). The routing-decision overhead itself: the traditional LLM-judge router costs a mean 369 micro-USD/call (a full generation call) vs the Jev diagnostic's 24 micro-USD/call -- a 15.4x difference for the routing step alone, isolated from generation cost. All 4 of the traditional router's false accepts were the identical phase 2 abstention-flag failure mode; the Jev gate caught and corrected all 4 of those same cases, because it checks evidence-presence directly rather than relying on generic claim-support/coverage verification to catch it indirectly. `classify_code_request_v1` is the one family where the adaptive `llm_judge_router` (120/140, $0.238444) beat both `jev_gated` and `fixed_strong` (which coincide at ~$0.535 since only strong cleared its 36-case pilot's quality floor) -- an honest limitation of a profile-based policy when its own pilot was too small to certify a cheaper tier, not a failure of the gate mechanism. See `artifacts/final/report.md` for full breakdowns.
- Cumulative OpenRouter spend to date: $4.400391 of the combined $42.30 authorized ($12.30 phases 1-3 + $30 phase 6, of which $3.857 spent).
